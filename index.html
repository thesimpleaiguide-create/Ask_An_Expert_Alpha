Lindsay Dubock • Conflict Management in General Practice (The Training Network)

90% of Conflict is Predictable

If conflict follows recognizable patterns, it can be prepared for. Training shifts staff from feeling like victims of conflict to skilled managers of it.

Module 1: Hidden Drivers & Locus of Control

  • In My Control (Accountable): My boundaries, thoughts, actions, responses, self-talk. (Stick like Velcro).
  • Out of My Control (Let Go): Others' choices, opinions, patient's anger, past/future. (Run off like Teflon — Monk carrying woman story).
  • Hidden Drivers: Aggression is overwhelmingly driven by Fear (illness, being dismissed, losing control, system delays), Shame/Embarrassment (literacy, addiction, social status), Past Trauma, and Social Factors.

Module 2: De-escalation, Fight-or-Flight & Miller's Law

  • Fight-or-Flight Neuroscience: Dysregulated nervous systems mirror each other. Healthcare staff must regulate first (calm, low, slow voice).
  • 3 Layers of Communication: 55% Body Language (45-degree stance, palms open, 2 arms' length), 38% Paraverbal (pitch, tone, pace), 7% Words.
  • Miller's Law (Judge vs. Detective): "To understand what someone is saying, assume it is true and try to imagine what it could be true of."
    • The Judge: Searches for inaccuracies ("Sir, it's only been 12 minutes!"). Escalates.
    • The Detective: Searches for underlying distress ("If it feels like an hour, they feel forgotten... You are a priority to us, let me check where the GP is"). De-escalates.

Module 3: MDE Behaviours (Manipulation, Demanding, Entitlement)

  • Core Driver: Regaining control when feeling vulnerable or powerless.
  • Manipulation: Indirect control (flattery, guilt, splitting staff, feigning vulnerability). Response: Stay warm but consistent. "Our team follows the same policy for every patient."
  • Demanding: Direct control (urgency, insistence). Response: Acknowledge emotion, offer 2 clear choices within policy.
  • Entitlement: Assumed control (superiority statements, name-dropping, exemption requests). Response: Frame fairness as the reason ("Our process is the same for every patient so everyone gets a fair standard of care").
  • Mantra: Calm dismantles manipulation. Consistency disarms entitlement. Fairness protects both patient and practice.

Module 4: The Boundaries Ladder

  • Boundary Definition: A condition ("I will continue to help you if..."), not a threat or refusal.
  • Boundary Formula: 1 Clear Limit + 1 Rationale + 1 Option
    Example: "I am not able to offer a same-day slot today (limit) because acute capacity is reserved for medical emergencies (rationale). What I can offer is a callback from the duty pharmacist or routine slot tomorrow (option)."
  • 5 Rungs: Rung 1 Clarify -> Rung 2 Contain -> Rung 3 Reaffirm -> Rung 4 Consequence ("If shouting continues, I will end this call") -> Rung 5 Conclude/Escalate. Start low, climb slowly, step back down when calm. Never re-argue a stated boundary.

Module 5: Managing Contacts Under Pressure

  • 4-Part Contact Structure: Open (role + purpose) -> Gather (1 focused question) -> Check (confirm key facts) -> Close (explicit plan by who/when + check understanding).
  • Pace Control & Skilled Redirect: Validate, name what you heard, move forward. ("Can I just pause you there — what is worrying you most right now?")
  • Not Taking the Bait: Respond to the presupposition/need, not the attack/accusation. (Child metaphor: reward the good, don't feed the bad).
  • Third-Party Callers: Keep patient at the center. Validate concern without breaching confidentiality or granting unvetted demands.
  • Clean Close: Confirm understanding aloud, state specific names/times (never "someone will be in touch"), safety net.
  • Defensible Documentation: Record objective facts, exact statements, and boundaries set — avoid emotional labels ("patient was rude").

Module 6: Resilience & Resetting

  • Resilience Definition: The ability to reset — not to endure abuse or cope alone.
  • Micro-Resilience Tools: Box Breathing (4-4-4-4 rhythm), Sensory Grounding, Silent Self-Talk ("This frustration is not about me. I am here to help, not to absorb their feelings").
  • Negativity Bias: Counteract by sharing positive feedback, debriefing objectively, and shifting language from "that was challenging, but we handled it."
